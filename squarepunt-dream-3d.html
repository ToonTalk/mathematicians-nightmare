Who wears what

The story fixes each number's uniform. The counts are worked out when the page loads, because they decide which uniform wins.

The first seven circles take the rainbow in order. 10, 100, 1,000 and so on are white. 13 and 666 are black.

A number of two kinds wears the uniform of the rarer kind among the first million. So 64 is a die, 100 is a white tile and 1,000 is a white die.

Where the story is silent, I chose. Plain numbers are pawn-shaped. Odd numbers (male) wear bowler hats and evens (female) wear tutus. “Round” is read as a multiple of ten. Pyramidal numbers (1, 5, 14, 30, 55…) have no uniform in the story, so they only form their own chain in the ballet. Numbers 1 to 1,000 are modelled one by one; the circles beyond are drawn as lines.
